// Global Credential-Harvesting Campaign · SOCRadar Labs Research

The Largest Credential-Harvesting Campaign Ever Documented Against Firewalls.

I discovered and documented the largest credential-harvesting operation ever found targeting network perimeter infrastructure. A custom adversary tool — written in Russian, compiled in Go — had silently compromised over 430,000 firewalls across 194 countries, passively intercepting authentication traffic across 24 different protocols without tripping a single alert.

The operation had harvested 110 million credentials — NTLM hashes, Kerberos tickets, cleartext passwords — from devices that were never meant to be a listening post. The disclosure triggered a CISA emergency alert and confirmed a breach at a NATO-aligned defense contractor. I mapped the full attack chain, from initial access through lateral movement to data exfiltration, so defenders had something they could actually act on.

ObjectiveDocument a large-scale adversary campaign against network perimeter infrastructure before more victims were silently compromised.
EnvironmentReal-world, internet-facing firewall/VPN infrastructure — independent research, SOCRadar Labs.
Adversary Access VectorCustom Go-based interception tool passively harvesting authentication traffic across 24 protocols.
Discovery MethodBehavioral and infrastructure analysis surfaced the tool's fingerprint across affected devices.
Attack PathPassive credential interception → NTLM/Kerberos/cleartext harvesting → silent exfiltration, no alerting triggered.
Impact430,000+ firewalls across 194 countries; confirmed breach at a NATO-aligned defense contractor.
Root CausePerimeter devices trusted as infrastructure rather than treated as monitored attack surface — no traffic inspection at the interception point.
RemediationCVE assigned, CISA emergency alert issued, affected organizations notified via coordinated disclosure.
LessonsA firewall is an attack surface, not just a control — perimeter infrastructure needs the same scrutiny as anything else exposed to the internet.

// RaaS Infrastructure Compromise · Resecurity HUNTER

We Broke Into the Ransomware Crew Before They Could Break You.

Known Aliases BlackLock El Dorado Eldorado Mamona

Most threat intelligence counts victims after the damage is done. We did the opposite. As part of the Resecurity HUNTER effort against BlackLock — a ransomware-as-a-service operation on track to become the most prolific of 2025, having grown its leak volume by 1,425% in a single quarter — the team identified a Local File Include flaw in the group's TOR data-leak site and turned it inward.

That foothold exposed their server configuration, credentials, and full command history from inside their own infrastructure. Their fatal mistake was mundane: reused passwords, copy-pasted straight into the shell, valid across multiple accounts. Operators who could ransom a government couldn't manage their own OPSEC.

ObjectiveIdentify and disrupt BlackLock's ransomware-as-a-service operation ahead of its next scheduled leak.
EnvironmentBlackLock's own TOR-hosted data-leak site infrastructure — authorized offensive research under Resecurity HUNTER.
Initial AccessLocal File Include (LFI) vulnerability identified in the group's TOR data-leak site.
DiscoveryFlaw surfaced during routine leak-site monitoring over the winter holiday window.
Attack PathLFI foothold → server config & credential exposure → command history capture → victim publication schedule intercepted.
Impact7 TB of stolen data recovered; victims warned days ahead of scheduled leaks; BlackLock and Mamona taken offline.
Root CauseReused, hardcoded passwords across the operator's own accounts — the same credential hygiene failure they exploited in victims.
DisruptionOPSEC failures exposed, affiliate trust collapsed, operator infrastructure taken offline and abandoned.

By March, BlackLock and Mamona were offline and the operator had vanished.
